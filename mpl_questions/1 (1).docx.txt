1. A batter scores 18 runs from 12 balls. If he maintains the same strike rate, how many runs will he score from 30 balls?
A) 40B) 42C) 45D) 48
Answer: C) 45
Solution:Strike rate = 18/12 × 100 = 150For 30 balls = 30 × 150/100 = 45 runs

A) 5.5B) 6C) 6.25D) 6.5
Answer: C) 6
Correction:Total runs = 19 + 5 = 24Economy = 24/4 = 6
 Correct Answer: B) 6
3. A team is chasing 173. They are 98/3 after 14 overs. How many runs must they score in the remaining 6 overs to win?
A) 74B) 75C) 76D) 77
Answer: B) 75
Solution:173 − 98 = 75 runs
4. A batter has scored 45 runs from 30 balls. He then scores 12 runs from the next 6 balls. What is his new strike rate?
A) 135B) 140C) 158.33D) 145
Answer: B) 158.33
Solution:Total runs = 45 + 12 = 57Total balls = 30 + 6 = 36
Strike rate = (57 ÷ 36) × 100= 158.33
5. A bowler concedes 8 runs in his first over. His economy after 3 overs is 6. How many runs did he concede in the next two overs combined?
A) 8B) 10C) 12D) 14
Answer: B) 10
Solution:Total after 3 overs = 6 × 3 = 18 runs.
First over = 8 runs.
Next two overs = 18 − 8 = 10 runs.

A) 48B) 50C) 52D) 54
Answer: C) 52
Solution:Boundary runs = 35% of 80 = 28Non-boundary runs = 80 − 28 = 52
7.A batter scores 32, 46 and 58 in three matches. How many runs must he score in the fourth match to have an average of 50?
A) 54B) 60C) 64D) 68
Answer: c) 64
Solution:Required total = 50 × 4 = 200
Current total = 32 + 46 + 58 = 136
Fourth score = 200 − 136 = 64
✅ Correct answer: C) 64
8.A box contains 4 cricket balls, 3 tennis balls and 5 table-tennis balls. One ball is selected randomly. What is the probability that it is not a cricket ball?
A) 1/3B) 2/3C) 3/4D) 5/8
Answer: B) 2/3
Solution:Total balls = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12
Non-cricket balls = 3 + 5 = 8
Probability = 8/12 = 2/3.

13Q. The first row of a stadium stand has 20 seats. Each successive row has 4 more seats than the previous row. If there are 10 rows, how many seats are there in total?
A) 360B) 380C) 400D) 420
Answer: B) 380
5-line solution:
First term = 20 and common difference = 4. 
Number of rows = 10. 
Last row = 20 + 9(4) = 56. 
Sum = 10/2 × (20 + 56) = 380. 
Therefore, total seats = 380.

What is the probability that both balls are of different colours?
A) 47/66B) 49/66C) 51/66D) 53/66
Answer: A) 47/66
Solution:Total ways to choose 2 balls:
¹²C₂ = 66
Same-colour pairs:
Red: ⁵C₂ = 10Blue: ⁴C₂ = 6Green: ³C₂ = 3
Total same-colour = 19
Different-colour pairs = 66 − 19 = 47
Probability = 47/66.

21Q. A rectangular cricket ground is 140 m long and 100 m wide. A uniform 5 m-wide walking track is constructed inside the boundary on all four sides.
What is the area occupied by the walking track?
A) 2,100 m²B) 2,300 m²C) 2,500 m²D) 2,700 m²
Answer: B) 2,300 m²
Solution:Original area = 140 × 100 = 14,000 m²
Inner dimensions:Length = 140 − 10 = 130 mWidth = 100 − 10 = 90 m
Inner area = 130 × 90 = 11,700 m²
Track area = 14,000 − 11,700 = 2,300 m²
